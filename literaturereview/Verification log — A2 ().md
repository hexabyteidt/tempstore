## Eligibility/design findings

The exact taxon is **Cannabis sativa L.** The experiment used two legal industrial hemp varieties: monoecious Futura 75 and dioecious Tisza (Materials and methods, “Experimental site design,” PDF p. 115416). It was a genuine **on-site field experiment**, not a pot or greenhouse study: working farmers’ fields in the Celje valley, Slovenia, were used. The three locations were selected by distance from the Cinkarna Celje zinc factory: HP about 1.7 km, MP about 3.4 km, and SP about 5.3 km (PDF p. 115416 and Fig. 1).

Contamination was **field-derived environmental contamination from zinc-ore smelting**, not experimental dosing. The paper says Celje is contaminated due to zinc ore smelting; more than 4,000 ha exceeded the Cd warning value and more than 50 ha exceeded the critical Cd value (PDF p. 115416). The plots were existing agricultural fields where food/feed crops are grown. Soil was sampled from the 0–20 cm plough layer as one composite per location from 30 random increments. Table 1 (PDF p. 115418) reports: HP Pb 98.2 ± 15.7, Zn 708 ± 113.3, Cd 6.6 ± 1.1 mg/kg soil; MP Pb 83.7 ± 13.4, Zn 227 ± 36.6, Cd 1.7 ± 0.3 mg/kg; SP Pb 41.4 ± 6.6, Zn 157 ± 25.1, Cd 1.1 ± 0.2 mg/kg. Soil pH (CaCl2) was HP 7.0 ± 0.3, MP 6.8 ± 0.3, SP 6.2 ± 0.2; organic matter was 4.9 ± 0.8%, 5.0 ± 0.9%, and 5.3 ± 0.9%, respectively.

The design was randomized complete block, 6 × 3 m basic plots, four replications per variety, with 144 m² total. Sowing was 12 May 2017 at 300 seeds/m²; harvest was 11 and 13 October 2017 (PDF pp. 115416–115417). The paper explicitly states that no fertilizers, herbicides, fungicides, or insecticides were used during the growing season (PDF p. 115416). There is no experimental amendment, chelation, spiking, artificial dosing, clean-soil dilution, hydroponic medium, or nutrient solution. Thus the unamended field design meets the locked contamination/design criterion.

Five plant components were analyzed: stems separated into bark and woody core, roots, inflorescences, and seeds. Dry samples were analyzed. Twenty-five stems were combined per sample; one composite soil sample and **three plant samples per tissue, variety, and site** were analyzed (PDF pp. 115416–115417). The analytical method was ICP-MS after aqua regia extraction for both soil and plant material (PDF p. 115417).

## BCF verification

The paper does **not** define or name a bioconcentration factor (BCF). A full-text search found no “BCF,” “bioconcentration factor,” or “bioaccumulation factor” term. Its reported **phytoremediation potential (PP)** is explicitly different: PP = tissue/organ content (mg/kg) × tissue/organ yield (kg/ha), Materials and methods, PDF p. 115416. PP values were therefore not relabeled as BCF.

However, the paper directly reports compatible dry-weight plant concentrations and compatible dry-weight soil concentrations, so plant/soil ratios can be calculated without using PP. For comparability with the locked primary convention, observations below use the **aboveground plant component concentration (mg/kg dry weight) / corresponding site soil concentration (mg/kg soil)**. These are explicitly marked **derived by verifier, not reported as BCF by the paper**. Only exact values stated in the prose and Table 1 were used; no values were read from plotted bars. The relevant plant values occur in the Pb results (PDF pp. 115419–115420), Zn results (PDF pp. 115421–115422), and Cd results (PDF pp. 115424–115425); soil denominators are from Table 1 (PDF p. 115418). Root-only values were not included because the locked comparable BCF convention is aboveground/soil.

## Independence and overlap

The exact DOI is absent from the supplied overlap audit’s seven confirmed primary-dataset DOI overlaps; the audit reports no probable-overlap flags. The paper is by Flajšman et al. (University of Ljubljana), uses the distinct Celje valley/Cinkarna Celje field sites, and has no author/site/sample-date indication linking it to Ha et al. 2025. The publication date, 26 October 2023, is after the stated 15 March 2023 Ha temporal cutoff. It is also not the Thurston Tar Creek hemp work (different authors, country, site, and design), not B2 (Phyllostachys bamboo), and not B5 (Thurston selenium/Tar Creek). Therefore it is treated as independent from Ha et al. 2025 and from the named Thurston/B2/B5 candidates.

## Final disposition

**INCLUDED.** The study satisfies the locked species, naturally contaminated field-soil, and no-spiking/no-amendment criteria. BCF is not directly named by the authors, but eligible aboveground/soil ratios are calculable from directly reported compatible dry-weight concentrations. The paper’s PP metric itself is not used as BCF.

## Caveats

The article reports many concentrations graphically (Figs. 5, 7, and 9; each caption gives n = 3), but this log does not digitize those figures. Consequently, the observation array is limited to plant concentration values explicitly stated in the article text and the exact soil values in Table 1. The calendar interval from 12 May to 11/13 October is approximately 152–154 days; the paper describes the growing season and dates but does not state a single duration in days.

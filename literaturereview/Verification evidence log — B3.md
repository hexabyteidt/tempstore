## 3. Soil origin and experimental design

The publisher abstract states that soil samples were collected from a chronosequence of bamboo stands cultivated for **0, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 10 years in Lin’an, Zhejiang Province, China**. This supports a field-cultivated stand chronosequence rather than hydroponics or a nutrient-solution experiment. The accessible abstract describes intensive management and evaluates changes in soil heavy-metal accumulation, chemical speciation, and bioavailability.

The abstract reports eight elements: **Cu, Zn, Pb, Ni, Cr, Cd, As, and Hg**. It states that Zn, Cu, Hg, and Cd increased with cultivation time, whereas Ni, Cr, Pb, and As were similar among stands. Multivariate/cluster analysis attributed Ni, Cr, Pb, and As likely to lithogenic sources and Zn, Cu, Hg, and Cd mainly to cultivation practices. This is evidence of field/background and management-associated contamination, not evidence of a laboratory spike.

However, the full methods were not available. Consequently, the following locked-design details cannot be fully verified from the primary source inspected here:

- Whether any stand received fertilizer, mulch, manure, or another exposure-changing input during the chronosequence;
- Whether the soil was ever experimentally spiked, diluted, or otherwise manipulated before sampling;
- Exact plot layout, number of stands/plots, sampling dates, replication, soil depth, and sample size;
- Whether bamboo shoots or other plant tissues were actually sampled and analyzed.

The abstract contains no statement of deliberate metal spiking, artificial dosing, clean-soil dilution, chelation, hydroponics, or nutrient solution. That absence is not treated as proof because the methods were inaccessible.

It does **not** state a BCF formula, plant-tissue numerator, soil denominator, BCF value, plant component, units, replicate count, or a table/figure containing plant and soil concentrations.

The article page’s accessible preview did not provide a full-text methods section, table, figure, or supplement from which a BCF could be directly read or calculated. Therefore, the following remain unavailable rather than inferred:

- BCF definition and numerator/denominator;
- Whether “plant concentration” means shoot, edible shoot, leaf, culm, root, or whole plant;

- BCF values, units, duration, and sample size;

The abstract’s statement that management “raised the potential risks of heavy metal pollution in bamboo shoots” is not a report of shoot concentrations or BCF and is not used as a numerical observation. No values are digitized or derived.

## 5. Eligibility decision

The species and broad field-soil setting appear compatible, and the abstract supports a non-hydroponic field chronosequence with lithogenic and cultivation-associated metal sources. Nevertheless, the locked dataset requires a directly reported BCF or a BCF calculable from directly reported compatible plant/soil concentrations. That requirement cannot be verified from the accessible primary-source material. Because the full article is paywalled/inaccessible here and the abstract does not establish plant-tissue BCF data, the study is **UNRESOLVED**, not included.

This is not marked `EXCLUDED` solely because the accessible record does not prove that plant data are absent; it is marked `UNRESOLVED` under the protocol’s rule for an inaccessible primary source and ambiguous BCF availability. If full text later confirms that the paper reports only soil-side measurements, it should be changed to `EXCLUDED` for no compatible BCF observation.

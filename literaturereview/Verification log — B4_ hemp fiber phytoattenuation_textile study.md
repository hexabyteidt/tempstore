# Verification log — B4: hemp fiber phytoattenuation/textile study

## Verdict

**Final status: INCLUDED.** The primary paper is De Vos et al. (2022), Industrial Crops and Products 178:114592, DOI [10.1016/j.indcrop.2022.114592](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.indcrop.2022.114592). It uses soil collected from an agricultural site beside the former Metaleurop Nord smelter (France), confirmed polluted with Cd, Pb and Zn, then used as field-derived soil in a controlled greenhouse experiment. The paper reports BCF for total aboveground biomass and plant parts; the BCF-total chart is directly primary-source evidence, but values below are **digitized from Figure 2** because the numeric chart data table is not supplied.

## Taxon, contamination origin, and design

- Species: *Cannabis sativa* L. (industrial hemp); six EU-registered cultivars: USO 31, Bialobrzeskie, Futura 75, Dacia Secuieni, Santhica 70, Carmagnola Selected.
- Soil origin: sandy loam from an agricultural site at 50°26′16.1″N, 3°00′53.3″E, approximately 0.5 km from the former Metaleurop Nord heavy-metal smelter in northern France. Methods §2.1 says the site is one of Europe’s heavily polluted sites and confirms contamination via soil characterization and legal sanitation limits. Table 1 reports total soil Cd 11.8 ± 0.1 mg/kg, Pb 664 ± 24 mg/kg, Zn 952 ± 10 mg/kg; pH 7.6 ± 0.0, OM 2.8 ± 0.1%, sandy loam (6.3% clay, 54.9% sand, 38.7% silt).
- No spiking, artificial dosing, clean-soil dilution, chelation, nutrient solution, hydroponics, biochar, or other exposure-changing amendment is described. Soil was collected, dried, sieved, homogenized, and placed into pots; this is field-derived naturally contaminated soil, not an artificial contamination treatment.
- Greenhouse design: climate-controlled ILVO greenhouse at Melle, Flanders; 6.5-L pots containing 5 kg air-dried soil; 10 replicate pots per cultivar (60 pots), randomized; one plant retained per pot after thinning; water maintained at 60% WHC. Growth approximately 70 days; harvest at 50% flowering: USO 31 56 d, Bialobrzeskie 63 d, Futura 75 and Dacia Secuieni 70 d, Santhica 70 and Carmagnola Selected 79 d.

## BCF definition and values

- Methods §2.5, p. 4: “The bioconcentration factor (BCF) was calculated for the total aboveground plant biomass using total and extractable HM concentrations in the soil”; Eq. (4) is BCF = Cx/Csoil, where Cx is concentration in the plant part (mg/kg) and Csoil is the corresponding soil HM concentration (mg/kg). The text says the calculation can be made for different plant parts and that BCF >1 indicates a hyperaccumulator, <1 an excluder. The paper distinguishes BCF calculations using total and extractable soil concentrations. Figure 2 is explicitly a BCF for total aboveground biomass (i.e., the plant numerator is weighted total aboveground biomass); the plotted heights numerically match the extractable-soil denominator in Table 1 (e.g., approximate Cd 0.63/0.087 ≈ 7.2, Pb ≈16.6/0.34 ≈48.7, Zn ≈29.2/1.8 ≈16.2), not the total-soil denominator. Therefore the Figure 2 observations below are reported as BCF with extractable/phytoavailable soil denominator, while the article’s equation text also mentions total-soil BCF but does not provide a separate numeric total-soil chart/table.
- Figure 2 (“BCF for total above ground biomass”) plots Cd, Pb, Zn for all six cultivars. The values below are digitized from the bar tops in the primary Figure 2 image; approximate to chart resolution, not invented or recomputed. The chart is the only numeric BCF source located. ## Independence and overlap audit

- Exact DOI is absent from the supplied confirmed-overlap DOI list and from the archived Ha et al. 2025 raw/analysis-ready BCF rows. However, Ha et al.’s archived supplementary citation table does contain this DOI as a citation/reference, marked not selected for their dataset; it also contains a separate 2023 De Vos field-cultivation paper (DOI 10.1007/s11356-023-25198-z). The present 2022 study is therefore not a DOI duplicate of Ha’s extracted observations.
- The 2022 paper’s experiment uses Metaleurop Nord-derived soil in an ILVO greenhouse and six cultivars; it is not the later 2023 Slovenia field-cultivation study, which uses two varieties and three Celje Valley sites. The 2022 and 2023 De Vos papers are related research-group outputs but have distinct soil sources/designs and are not the same experiment. No evidence connects B4 to the Thurston Tar Creek/B2/B5 candidates.

## Caveats

- Because Figure 2 is a plotted source, all observations are marked digitized and should retain chart-resolution uncertainty. The paper’s equation is compatible with a plant-concentration/soil-concentration BCF, but its methods mention both total and extractable denominators. Figure 2 labels “BCF for total above ground biomass”; matching chart heights to Table 1 and Figure 1 shows these plotted values use the extractable/phytoavailable soil denominator. No separate total-soil BCF values are primary-source verified. Retain this denominator distinction in downstream harmonization.

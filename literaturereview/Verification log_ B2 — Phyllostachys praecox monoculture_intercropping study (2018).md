# Verification log: B2 — *Phyllostachys praecox* monoculture/intercropping study (2018)

**Candidate study ID:** `phyllostachys_praecox_monoculture_2018`  
**Candidate label:** B2  
**Verification date:** 2026-09-06  
**Provisional final status:** **UNRESOLVED**  
**Reason for provisional status:** bibliographic identity and abstract-level design are verified from primary indexing/publisher records, but the article's methods, equations, full tables, and numerical BCF values were not accessible without the Taylor & Francis paywall/CAPTCHA. In addition, the article is from the same Bian/Zhong/Zhang/Yang research program as a confirmed-overlap bamboo study, and a shared-experiment determination cannot be made conclusively from the accessible abstract alone.

## 1. Primary-source identity

The candidate is a real, uniquely identified publication, not the unconfirmed placeholder DOI in the candidate list. PubMed PMID **28949764** gives the exact title, authors Fangyuan Bian, Zheke Zhong, Shengchun Wu, Xiaoping Zhang, Chuanbao Yang, and Xiaoyi Xiong; journal *International Journal of Phytoremediation*; volume 20, issue 5, pages 490–498; publication date 16 April 2018; and DOI **10.1080/15226514.2017.1374339** ([PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28949764/)). The publisher record independently gives the same DOI, authors, pages, and online publication date 2 April 2018 ([Taylor & Francis article page](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/15226514.2017.1374339)). Crossref confirms the same bibliographic metadata and the publisher PDF/full-text URLs ([Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1080/15226514.2017.1374339)).

The candidate's supplied `doi` value (`unconfirmed_verify_pubmed_28949764`) is therefore replaced for verification purposes by the confirmed DOI `10.1080/15226514.2017.1374339`.

## 2. Species/taxa and treatment arms

The PubMed and publisher abstracts explicitly identify **_Phyllostachys praecox_** as the bamboo and **_Sedum plumbizincicola_** as the Cd/Zn hyperaccumulator. They define three planting systems: bamboo monoculture (**MP**), *S. plumbizincicola* monoculture (**MS**), and *P. praecox × S. plumbizincicola* intercropping (**IPS**). The candidate's bamboo-only arm is therefore identifiable at abstract level as MP. *Phyllostachys* is the same bamboo genus used by the locked bamboo terminology; however, no assumption is made that this species is interchangeable with *P. pubescens* in the related paper below.

The abstract says the systems were established in **Cu-, Zn-, and Cd-contaminated soil** and that soil and plant heavy-metal contents were assessed after **four years of plantation establishment**. It does not identify the contamination source, field location, exact planting dates, plot dimensions, replicate count, or whether the soil was experimentally spiked or diluted.

## 3. Contamination origin and field/greenhouse design

The article abstract uses only “Cu, Zn, and Cd-contaminated soil.” It does **not** say “naturally contaminated,” “field-derived,” “mining soil,” or “spiked,” and the accessible publisher page is abstract-only because the full article is behind access control. Consequently, natural contamination and absence of artificial dosing are **not primary-source verified** for this article.

A separate Chinese patent by a related research group describes a *P. praecox*–*S. plumbizincicola* field remediation experiment in Fuyang District, Hangzhou (119°53′E, 29°52′N), on soil reportedly contaminated after approximately ten years of industrial wastewater irrigation; it gives 2012 planting, May 2016 sampling, 10 m × 20 m plots, and MP/MS/IPS terminology. This is **not the candidate article** and is retained only as corroborative overlap context, not as a substitute for the article's methods: [CN107737802A, Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/CN107737802A/zh). The patent also describes organic-fertilizer management in its claimed remediation method, so it cannot establish that the candidate article had no amendment.

The related confirmed-overlap paper by the same Bian/Zhong/Zhang/Yang lineage reports a separate title/species combination—*Phyllostachys pubescens* with *S. plumbizincicola*—and the same Cu/Zn/Cd, monoculture/intercropping framing ([PubMed PMID 28965200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28965200/); [Springer page](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11356-017-0326-2)). Its abstract also reports assessment after four years. Because the bamboo taxa differ (*P. praecox* versus *P. pubescens*), this is evidence of close research-lineage/design overlap but not, by itself, proof that the same experimental units were reused.

## 4. Amendments, spiking, dilution, or other exposure manipulation

No artificial spike, clean-soil dilution, chelator, hydroponic medium, nutrient solution, or exposure-changing amendment is mentioned in the accessible candidate abstract. This is an absence from the abstract, **not a verified negative finding**. The full methods are required to determine whether the “contaminated soil” was naturally field-derived and used as-is, and whether fertilizer or another management input was applied. Under the locked protocol, this uncertainty prevents inclusion.

The intercropping arm is an exposure/system manipulation, but the candidate list correctly identified the MP monoculture arm as the only potentially comparable bamboo-only condition. No MP numeric rows are extracted because the article's tables and formula are not accessible.

## 5. BCF/bioconcentration-factor verification

The PubMed and publisher abstracts state that “the bioconcentration and the translocation factors in most of bamboo tissues showed an increasing trend from the MP to the IPS.” This verifies that the article discusses bioconcentration factors at a general level, but it does **not** provide:

* the BCF equation or the authors' exact numerator/denominator convention;
* whether the numerator is root, rhizome, branch, leaf, shoot, whole plant, or another tissue;
* dry-weight versus fresh-weight basis;
* any numeric BCF value;
* compatible plant and soil concentration pairs from which a BCF could be calculated; or
* table/figure/page locations for individual values.

The publisher's full-text page exposes only the abstract and reports that access requires institutional login or purchase; the PDF route was blocked by publisher CAPTCHA in the available browser session. The accessible publisher “Figures & data” page likewise exposed metadata and abstract text but no readable tables. Therefore **all BCF observations remain missing**. No value is digitized, inferred from a trend, reconstructed from a review, or borrowed from the related Bian paper.

## 6. Independence and shared-experiment audit

### 6.1 Exact DOI overlap with the primary/Ha dataset

The local overlap audit reports seven confirmed DOI overlaps and no probable-overlap flags. The candidate's confirmed DOI `10.1080/15226514.2017.1374339` is **not** among the seven confirmed DOI overlaps. Thus there is no exact DOI match to the primary/Ha dataset on the available audit.

That DOI result is not sufficient to establish independence. The candidate shares the first, second, and fourth authors and the same bamboo/Sedum intercropping program with the confirmed-overlap paper DOI **10.1007/s11356-017-0326-2**, which is already in the primary dataset. The two papers also share the Cu/Zn/Cd soil framing, monoculture/intercropping structure, BCF/TF endpoint family, and four-year plantation framing. The candidate uses *P. praecox* and adds Wu/Xiong, whereas the confirmed-overlap paper uses *P. pubescens* and has Bian/Zhong/Zhang/Yang. This makes a shared research lineage clear but leaves same experimental units unresolved.

### 6.2 Independence from Ha et al. 2025

No exact DOI overlap with the Ha/primary dataset was found in the supplied overlap audit. The authors, institutions, field site, and sample-collection dates of Ha et al. 2025 could not be compared directly because the candidate full methods were inaccessible. The best supported statement is therefore: **no exact DOI overlap; no evidence in the accessible record of the Ha group or Ha field site; independence is plausible but not fully verified.**

### 6.3 Independence from Thurston/B5 candidates

There is no indication that this Chinese *Phyllostachys*/*Sedum* study shares soil, plants, or experimental units with the Thurston Tar Creek hemp candidates. Those candidates concern *Cannabis sativa* and naturally contaminated Tar Creek Superfund soil in the United States, whereas this candidate concerns a Chinese bamboo/Sedum program. This is a strong contextual basis for independence from Thurston/B5, but it does not resolve the internal Bian bamboo overlap described above.

## 7. Eligibility decision

The study is relevant in species and general soil-system terms, but it is not eligible for a verified observation row at this stage because the locked requirements cannot be completed from the accessible primary-source material: natural/field-derived contamination is not explicitly verified; no-spiking/no-exposure-changing-amendment status is not verified; the exact BCF definition is unavailable; numeric BCF values or compatible concentration pairs are unavailable; and shared-experiment status with the already included Bian bamboo paper remains unresolved.

The correct frozen-candidate status is **UNRESOLVED**, not INCLUDED. It should not contribute any BCF observations until the full article PDF or author manuscript is obtained and the methods/tables are inspected. If the full methods demonstrate that the candidate's MP plots are the same experimental campaign or samples as DOI 10.1007/s11356-017-0326-2, the final status should be changed to **DUPLICATE_SHARED_STUDY**. If they are demonstrably separate field plots and the soil was naturally contaminated and unamended, the paper could be reconsidered for inclusion after direct extraction of the MP BCF rows.

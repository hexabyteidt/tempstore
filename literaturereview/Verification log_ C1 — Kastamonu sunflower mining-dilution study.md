# Verification log: C1 — Kastamonu sunflower mining-dilution study

## Decision

**Final status: EXCLUDED.** The article is a real primary study with eligible taxon and naturally mining-derived soil, and it directly reports a BAF that is numerically compatible with a shoot/soil BCF. However, the experiment is a greenhouse pot dilution series: contaminated mine-dump soil was deliberately mixed with clean forest soil at 0%, 50%, and 100%. This imposed exposure-intensity manipulation is disallowed by the locked eligibility rule excluding primarily exposure-changing amendments/dilutions. No observation is therefore carried into the eligible external-validation array.

## Identity and source verification

The publisher page identifies the article as Mustafa Aybar, Bülent Sağlam, Hatice Dağhan, Aydın Tüfekçıoğlu, Nurcan Kölelı, and Fatma Nur Yılmaz, “Phytoextraction of Heavy Metal (Cu, Zn, Pb) from Mining Area by Sunflower (*Helianthus annuus*),” *Kastamonu University Journal of Forestry Faculty*, 23(1), 75–85 (2023), DOI **10.17475/kastorman.1269527** [1]. The publisher records publication on 24 March 2023; the PDF gives received 20 October 2022 and accepted 17 March 2023 [1] [2]. Crossref independently returns the same DOI, title, author list, journal, volume, pages, and publication date [3].

The primary source is the publisher’s full article PDF, not a snippet, review, or secondary summary [2]. The PDF is 11 pages; the printed article pages are 75–85. No supplement was listed or needed for the reported BAF table.

## Taxon and elements

The plant material is explicitly identified as the Oliva CL hybrid sunflower variety, with seeds obtained from the Thrace Agricultural Research Institute. The paper identifies sunflower as *Helianthus annuus* L. [2, PDF printed p. 78, “Plant Material”]. The measured elements are copper (Cu), zinc (Zn), and lead (Pb), reported in the abstract, methods, soil Table 2, plant Table 3, and BAF/TF Table 5 [1] [2, printed pp. 75, 78–81]. The occasional Turkish-title transcription “Cd” is inconsistent with the English title and the methods/results; the numerical experiment is explicitly Cu/Zn/Pb throughout the primary source.

## Contamination origin and design

The contamination is field-derived and mining-related. The authors state that contaminated and uncontaminated soils were collected from forest-area points in the Artvin–Murgul rust/waste dumping area and the Artvin–Sachinka region, at 0–30 cm depth; coordinates are given as 41°13′17″ N, 41°50′39″ E for Artvin–Murgul and 41°14′21″ N, 41°33′16″ E for Artvin–Sachinka [2, printed p. 77, “Study Area and Soil Material,” and Figure 1]. The pot-trial methods specify that high-metal soil came from the rust dumping area around the Artvin–Murgul mine site and low-metal soil came from the forest area within the Artvin Forestry Operations Directorate/Sacinka Forestry Directorate [2, printed p. 78, “Pot Trial”]. This supports naturally contaminated/field-derived source soil, not a synthetic substrate.

The cultivation itself was **not field-based**. It was conducted in the Artvin Çoruh University research greenhouse [1] [2, abstract]. Air-dried soils were passed through a 1-mm sieve and mixed at 0%, 50%, and 100% ratios “due to the excessive pollution” of the mine-site soil. The mixtures were incubated for one month, then 10 kg was placed in 14-kg pots [2, printed p. 78, “Pot Trial”]. The 0% mixture was forest soil, the 50% mixture was a forest/mine-soil mixture, and the 100% mixture was mine-dump soil [2, printed p. 79, paragraph immediately before Table 2]. The experiment used a completely randomized block design with four replicates. Ten seeds were sown per pot and thinned to one plant per pot after germination. Plants were grown for five weeks under controlled greenhouse conditions of 50–60% humidity and 24 ± 2 °C, then harvested and separated into green parts and roots [2, printed p. 78].

There is no report of metal spiking, chelation, nutrient solution, or hydroponics. The disqualifying exposure manipulation is the deliberate dilution/mixing of contaminated soil with clean/low-metal forest soil across the 0%/50%/100% series. The paper’s own conclusion also recommends that future work be tested in field conditions rather than the present controlled environment [2, printed p. 81, BAF/TF discussion at lines immediately before “Conclusions”].

## Soil and plant measurements

Table 1 reports the soil properties by pollution level: pH 7.04, 6.42, and 5.85; EC 0.52, 0.84, and 1.02 dS/m; CaCO3 3.50%, 2.76%, and 1.99%; organic matter 3.89%, 2.41%, and 0.64%; and texture classes sandy loam, sandy loam, and loamy sand for 0%, 50%, and 100%, respectively [2, printed p. 79, Table 1].

Table 2 reports total soil metal concentrations at trial start and after harvest. Beginning concentrations (mg kg−1) were:

| Soil level | Cu | Zn | Pb |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 76.9 ± 12.7 | 95.9 ± 10.4 | 70.6 ± 8.7 |
| 50% | 493 ± 39.5 | 233 ± 36.9 | 115 ± 28.8 |
| 100% | 1102 ± 47.3 | 324 ± 38.7 | 230 ± 46.9 |

The corresponding end-of-harvest concentrations were 61.7 ± 14.6, 64.3 ± 14.1, and 37.6 ± 5.2 mg kg−1 for Cu/Zn/Pb at 0%; 395 ± 38.6, 102 ± 12.6, and 62.9 ± 12.4 mg kg−1 at 50%; and 900 ± 37.5, 213 ± 42.9, and 77.1 ± 15.8 mg kg−1 at 100% [2, printed p. 79, Table 2].

Table 3 reports green-part and root concentrations in mg kg−1. Green-part Cu/Zn/Pb were 50.25/92.50/49.13 at 0%, 63.13/123.60/55.75 at 50%, and 79.38/214.40/74.00 at 100%. Root Cu/Zn/Pb were 50.13/65.50/50.88 at 0%, 172.60/107.80/58.88 at 50%, and 450.00/179.10/72.63 at 100% [2, printed p. 80, Table 3]. The plant samples were washed, dried at 65 °C to constant weight, ground, acid-digested, and analyzed by ICP-OES [2, printed p. 78].

## BAF/BCF definition and reported values

The paper calls the metric **bioaccumulation factor (BAF)** rather than BCF. It defines BAF as the ratio of heavy-metal concentration in shoots to heavy-metal concentration in soil and gives the equation:

> “BAF = Shoot Metal Concentration (mg kg−1) / Soil Metal Concentration (mg kg−1)” [2, printed p. 79, Eq. 1].

This is compatible with a shoot/soil BCF definition, with both numerator and denominator reported on a dry-mass concentration basis in mg kg−1; the resulting ratio is dimensionless. The paper’s Table 5 directly reports the following BAF values [2, printed p. 81, Table 5]:

| Soil level | Cu BAF | Zn BAF | Pb BAF |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0% | 0.654 | 0.965 | 0.696 |
| 50% | 0.128 | 0.531 | 0.485 |
| 100% | 0.072 | 0.661 | 0.322 |
| Mean | 0.284 | 0.719 | 0.501 |

The values are directly reported, not digitized. They are also reproducible from the directly reported beginning soil concentrations in Table 2 and green-part concentrations in Table 3; for example, 50.25/76.9 = 0.654 for Cu at 0%, 214.40/324 = 0.661 for Zn at 100%, and 74.00/230 = 0.322 for Pb at 100%, subject to table rounding. Table 5 also reports TF, but TF is a shoot/root ratio and is not treated as BCF [2, printed pp. 79 and 81].

These BAF values are **not eligible observations** because every value belongs to the deliberate 0%/50%/100% soil-dilution experiment. The 100% arm is the undiluted mine-dump soil, but it remains one arm of the controlled greenhouse dilution design rather than an independent ambient field observation. Under the locked rule, isolating that arm would not remove the study-level exposure-manipulation confound; therefore no rows are emitted.

## Independence and duplicate/shared-study checks

The supplied overlap audit reports seven confirmed DOI overlaps and zero probable-overlap flags. DOI **10.17475/kastorman.1269527** is not among the confirmed-overlap DOIs [4]. The candidate is also temporally outside Ha et al.’s literature-search/data-collection window: Ha et al. state that their search ran from 1 August 2022 through 15 March 2023 [5]. The candidate was accepted on 17 March 2023 and published on 24 March 2023 [1] [2], so it was not available by the stated Ha search cutoff. A full-text search of the Ha article PDF found no occurrence of the candidate DOI, Aybar, Artvin, Murgul, Kastamonu, or Sachinka. The authors, institution, field location, and experimental design are distinct from Ha et al.’s dataset-construction article. **Assessment: independent from Ha et al. 2025 = yes, with no evidence of shared experimental units.**

The study is also distinct from the inspected Thurston/B1/B5 candidates: those are *Cannabis sativa* work associated with Tar Creek Superfund soil in Oklahoma, whereas C1 is *Helianthus annuus* grown in an Artvin–Murgul/Sachinka soil-dilution experiment in Türkiye. There is no shared author, institution, site, soil source, species, or experimental-unit evidence in the inspected records. C1 is also distinct from B2, the *Phyllostachys praecox*/*Sedum plumbizincicola* Chinese bamboo program, which uses different taxa, authors, geographic context, and field program. **Assessment: no evidence of a shared experiment with Thurston/B1/B5 or B2.**

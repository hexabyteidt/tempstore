## 2. Species/taxon

The target is explicitly *Helianthus annuus* L. (sunflower), hybrid Pioneer P10. The publisher abstract and PDF Section 2.1 identify sunflower as the current rotation crop; PDF Section 2.2 (pp. 4–5) identifies the collected plants as sunflower and lists the eight sampled organs: roots, stems, leaves, petioles, receptacles, ray florets, disc florets, and seeds. This is exactly within the locked sunflower taxon.

## 3. Contamination origin and eligibility design

The study is a field-derived arable-soil study, not a pot, greenhouse, hydroponic, nutrient-solution, or artificial-spike experiment. PDF Section 2.1 (pp. 3–4) states that the site is an arable field in the Ayet rural okrug, Beimbet Mailin district, Kostanay region, Northern Kazakhstan (52.7927° N, 62.6876° E; 197 m a.s.l.). The crop was grown in the field during the 2025 rotation and plants were excavated in August 2025 at physiological maturity.

The source explicitly characterizes the site as a reference/background agroecosystem rather than a pronounced anthropogenic-contamination site: it is approximately 50 km from the Sokolov-Sarbai Mining Production Association; “there is no record of industrial, mining, or waste activity in the area”; and the site is described as representative of background/unimpacted agroecosystems. The paper’s geochemical interpretation attributes Cd, Co, and As anomalies to lithogenic/geogenic origin, while Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn are below upper-continental-crust Clarke values. PDF Abstract; Section 2.1 pp. 3–4; Section 3.2 pp. 9–10; Section 4 conclusions pp. 24–25.

This satisfies the locked **field-derived natural-soil** condition: the soil and plants were sampled in situ from a real agricultural field and no artificial contamination was imposed. It should not be described as a heavily polluted or mining-soil experiment; it is a natural/background exposure observation. The final decision treats “naturally contaminated/field-derived soil” as including field-derived natural-background soil, while preserving this caveat explicitly.

## 4. Field design, amendments, and exposure manipulation

This was a single-site, single-season field characterization. Sunflower was sown 10 May 2025. The paper reports conventional field management: ammonium nitrate (80 kg ha−1, equivalent to 27.4 kg N ha−1) at sowing; post-emergence tribenuron-methyl and clethodim; and insecticides/fungicides with foliar boron fertilizer at BBCH 51. It explicitly states that no additional fertilizers were applied before harvest and that phosphorus fertilizer, manure, and sewage sludge were not applied (PDF Section 2.1, pp. 3–4).

There was no spiking, artificial dosing, clean-soil dilution, chelation, hydroponics, nutrient solution, or primarily exposure-changing amendment. The conventional crop-management inputs were not imposed to alter metal exposure and do not constitute an exposure-manipulation arm. There are no treatment groups or amendment-rate comparisons.

## 5. Soil and plant sampling

PDF Section 2.3 (pp. 5–6) reports 18 soil samples from the complete 0–150+ cm profile: six horizons (Ap1 0–5 cm, Ap2 5–26 cm, AB 26–71/76 cm, Bk 71/76–83 cm, BCk 83–122 cm, Ck 122–150+ cm), with three lateral replicates per horizon (left, central, and right walls of the profile pit). The BCF denominator is the plow/root-zone interval Ap1 + Ap2, 0–26 cm.

PDF Section 2.2 (pp. 4–5) reports six randomly selected field plants collected in August 2025 at physiological maturity. Plant material was organized into the eight organs listed above. PDF Section 2.6 (p. 8) reports plant results as mean ± SD of three biological replicates, each replicate being a composite of two whole sunflower plants (n = 3 replicates; six plants total). Plant tissues were oven-dried at 70 °C to constant mass, digested with HNO3/H2O2, and quantified by ICP-OES. Plant concentration units are mg kg−1 dry weight.

Exact harvest date and therefore exact duration in days are **not reported**. Sowing date and collection month are reported, but no duration is imputed.

## 6. BCF definition and compatibility

The paper directly reports compatible BCF values in Table 7 (PDF p. 17; Section 3.7, pp. 17–18). The explicit methods equation is PDF Section 2.5, p. 7, Eq. (2):

> “BCF = Croot / Csoil,” where Croot is element concentration in roots (mg kg−1 dry weight) and Csoil is the corresponding pseudo-total concentration (mg kg−1) in the plow layer (0–26 cm, combined Ap1 and Ap2 horizons).

Section 3.7 states that soil concentrations were depth-weighted means for Ap1 + Ap2 (0–26 cm), and that shoot concentration was a biomass-weighted mean across seven aboveground organs: disc florets, seeds, receptacle, ray florets, leaves, petioles, and stems. Table 7 labels the reported ratios as BCF root/soil and BCF shoot/soil. Thus, the observations below use the **pseudo-total** denominator, which is the compatible total-soil convention; the separately reported “mobile” BCF columns are not extracted as primary observations because they use an ammonium-acetate mobile-fraction denominator rather than the locked total-soil convention.

Table 7 directly reports pseudo-total BCF values for Cd, Co, Cr, Cu, Ni, Pb, and Zn. As was quantified in soil, but all plant-organ As concentrations were below the ICP-OES detection limit and As was excluded from plant-related analysis (PDF Section 3.5, pp. 15–16); consequently, no As BCF observation is created.

Table 8 (PDF p. 21) reports the corresponding depth-weighted Ap1–Ap2 pseudo-total soil concentrations used for the plant/soil indices: Cd 0.22, Co 34.50, Cr 37.22, Cu 14.32, Ni 27.03, Pb 12.02, and Zn 33.41 mg kg−1. The BCF values themselves are directly reported in Table 7, so the soil concentration field is provenance context, not a user-derived reconstruction. Rounded Table 8 concentrations should not be expected to reproduce every Table 7 ratio exactly.

## 10. Final decision and caveats

**Final status: INCLUDED.** The study is a primary, identity-verified, field-derived natural-soil study of a locked target species; it has no spiking or exposure-changing amendment arm; and it directly reports compatible pseudo-total-soil BCF values in Table 7. The site is explicitly a clean/background reference agroecosystem rather than a heavily anthropogenically contaminated site. This natural-background status, exact duration unavailability, and exclusion of mobile-denominator BCF columns are retained as caveats rather than filled by inference.

No BCF is extracted for As because plant As was below detection and the paper excluded As from plant-related analysis. No duration in days is imputed from “August 2025” or from the 10 May sowing date.
